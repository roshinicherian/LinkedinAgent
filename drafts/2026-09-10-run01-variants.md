## VARIANT A — hook formula F17 (Controlled A/B Anecdote) — goal: comments

**Title: The Brief Does the Work**

The draft came back in ninety seconds and it read beautifully. Every sentence true, and not one of them a sentence a buyer would need.

It described a piece of industrial equipment the way the brochure already did. So I threw it out and wrote the brief again.

Same product. Same model. Same twenty minutes. The only thing I added was the list of objections the sales team hears on every call.

The second draft opened with the thing that fails first in the field.

That gap is wider in technical categories than anywhere else I work. The person reading a spec page has usually specified this category before. They know the tolerances, and they can tell inside a sentence whether the writer has been on site or only on the website.

AI writes fluently at exactly the level of generality that reads as never having been on site. It doesn't know what a plant manager is quietly worried about at 4pm on a Friday, and it won't think to ask.

So the work moved rather than disappeared. I spend longer on briefs now and almost no time rewriting drafts, and most of that brief is questions I had to put to someone who actually does the job.

## VARIANT B — hook formula F16 (Status-Strip Humility) — goal: likes / warmth

**Title: The Forty-Second Edit**

Ten years of writing B2B copy, and the best edit I've ever been handed took about forty seconds and came from someone who doesn't write for a living.

I'd sent a paragraph on how a component performed under load. Technically accurate, carefully hedged, quietly proud of itself.

The sales engineer read it once, crossed the whole thing out, and wrote a single sentence about the failure it prevents. His version was better because he'd been called out to that failure at 2am.

That moment turns up every time I brief AI on a technical product now. The draft comes back articulate and slightly hollow, because the model has read a thousand pages about the category and never stood next to the thing when it fails.

Most buyers in these categories aren't AI-native either. The ABS put business AI use in Australia at 12% in 2024-25, up from 1% three years earlier, with IT, media and telcos leading adoption. The categories I work in aren't in that leading group.

So generic reads as generic far faster here than in software.

AI doesn't write my first drafts anymore when a specialist will read them. It interrogates my brief, and I take the questions to the person who knows.

Who's the person outside marketing whose edits you trust most?
