# Run 01 — 2026-09-10 — Pillar 1: AI in marketing practice

Topic: briefing AI on B2B copy for technical products, where the buyer knows the
product better than the writer does.

Week goal: **comments**. Variant A is built for it; Variant B trades some comment
pull for warmth and carries the verified stat.

---

## VARIANT A — hook formula F17 (Controlled A/B Anecdote) — goal: comments

Two briefs for the same product page. One came back usable. One came back as a brochure.

The second brief had one extra line: the objections the sales team hears on every call.

Same product. Same writer. Same twenty minutes. The only variable was whether the AI knew what the buyer was sceptical about.

For technical products that gap is wider than anywhere else I work. The person reading a spec page has usually specified this category before. They know the tolerances, and they can tell inside a sentence whether the writer has been on site or only on the website.

AI writes fluently at exactly the level of generality that reads as never having been on site. It doesn't know what a plant manager is quietly worried about, and it won't think to ask.

So the work moved rather than disappeared. I spend longer on the brief now and almost no time rewriting the draft, and most of that brief is questions I had to put to someone who actually does the job.

## VARIANT B — hook formula F16 (Status-Strip Humility) — goal: likes / warmth

Ten years into B2B marketing, and the fastest edit I ever got came from a sales engineer who doesn't write for a living.

I'd written a paragraph about how a component performed under load. Technically accurate. He crossed the whole thing out and wrote one sentence about the failure it prevents. His version was better and it took him about forty seconds.

That gap turns up every time I brief AI on a technical product now. The draft comes back articulate and slightly hollow, because the model has read a thousand pages about the category and has never stood next to the thing when it fails.

Worth remembering that most buyers in these categories aren't AI-native either. The ABS put business AI use in Australia at 12% in 2024-25, up from 1% three years earlier, with IT, media and telcos leading adoption. The categories I work in aren't in that leading group.

So generic reads as generic much faster here than it does in software.

AI doesn't write the first draft of anything a specialist will read for me anymore. It interrogates my brief instead, and I take the questions to the person who knows.

Who's the person outside marketing whose edits you trust most?
