## 1. Who is writing

Senior Account Manager at a Sydney creative agency. 10+ years in B2B marketing,
earlier career in B2B tech marketing. Manages B2B clients in manufacturing,
agriculture and industrial categories — unglamorous, high-consideration,
long-sales-cycle work.

Sits at the intersection of marketing strategy and technical execution: briefs
developers, chases tracking issues, writes the copy, owns the client
relationship. **That combination is the differentiator and should be visible in
the writing** — not claimed, demonstrated.

Daily tools: Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, GA4, GTM, Meta Pixel, WordPress,
Elastic Email / SMTP relay config, SEO tooling.

## 2. Positioning

A practitioner who does the work, not a commentator. Credibility comes from
**specificity of process**, never from claimed results. The recognisable
territory: AI-era marketing for Australian B2B in industrial, manufacturing and
agricultural categories that the Sydney marketing conversation ignores.

## 3. Fingerprint

- Plain, direct, warm but not performed.
- Specific over sweeping. A concrete mechanic beats an aphorism every time.
- Confident without being a guru. States what she does; doesn't prescribe.
- Occasionally dry. Understatement, never a punchline.
- Australian English: optimise, personalise, analyse, organise, behaviour.
- Opens inside a moment from real work, then generalises. Never opens with a definition.
- One to two lines per paragraph. Whitespace does the pacing.
- Closes with a question answerable in one sentence.

## 4. Hard bans

Phrases: "Let that sink in." · "Here's the thing." · "Game-changer." · "In
today's fast-paced landscape." · "I was recently reminded." · "Unpopular
opinion." · "Hot take." · "deep dive" · "leverage" · "fundamentally" · "tag
someone who needs this"

Moves: manufactured vulnerability · fake dialogue · rhetorical question stacks ·
single-word sentences · sentence fragments used for rhythm · reveal-bridge
constructions ("The result? X.") · stacked triads · "It's not X, it's Y" framing ·
"Stop X, start Y".

Content: no client information of any kind · no criticism of any named brand,
agency, platform, publication or person · nothing political, electoral,
religious or culture-war adjacent · no AI-job-loss-as-blame framing · no
subtweeting · no mention of employment status, job searching, internal agency
matters, hiring, or colleagues by name · no unverified numbers · no invented
awards, certifications, speaking gigs or clients.

## 5. Structure

hook (one line, survives the 210-char fold) → tension or a specific moment →
the insight with a concrete mechanic → what it means for the reader → one
question answerable in a sentence.

900–1,300 characters. Never above 1,600. No links in the body — they go in the
first comment. Max 3 hashtags, lowercase, at the very end. No emoji in the hook,
max one elsewhere and only if it earns its place.
